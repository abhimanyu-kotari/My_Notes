Functional Grammar & Tenses for Speaking

Now that you have the basic S-V-O structure, it's time to add natural color and precision. In this volume, you will master the visual prepositions map (In vs. On vs. At), learn how to talk about ownership without tripping over pronouns, transform blunt commands into elegant polite requests, and conquer tenses (Past, Present, and Future) for storytelling without memorizing boring grammar charts!

00. The Goal of Phase 2: From Broken Sentences to Smooth Flow

In Week 1, you learned how to put 3 words together: "I code software."

In Week 2, you will learn how to expand that sentence with context, time, place, and polite manners:

Level 1 (Week 1):  "I work."
Level 2 (Preposition): "I work at a software company in Bangalore."
Level 3 (Time/Tense): "I have been working there since last year."
Level 4 (Polite Speech): "Could you please share your feedback on my project?"

08. Day 08: Prepositions of Place & Time (In, On, At)

Prepositions tell us WHERE something is located or WHEN something happens. Beginners often confuse In, On, and At. Here is the visual pyramid rule:

📐 The Preposition Inverted Pyramid

IN — Biggest / Most General
Time: Centuries, Decades, Years, Months, Seasons (in 2026, in December, in summer)
Place: Countries, Cities, Enclosed Rooms (in India, in Mumbai, in the room, in a car)
ON — Medium / Specific Surfaces & Days
Time: Specific Days & Dates (on Monday, on my birthday, on 15th August)
Place: Surfaces, Streets, Public Transit (on the table, on MG Road, on the bus, on the train)
AT — Pinpoint / Exact Point
Time: Exact Clock Time (at 5:00 PM, at midnight, at lunchtime)
Place: Specific Address / Spot (at the door, at home, at the airport)
The Vehicle Rule (In vs. On):
Can you stand up and walk inside the vehicle? If YES → use ON (on the bus, on the train, on the plane, on the ship).
Must you crouch/sit inside without walking? If YES → use IN (in the car, in the taxi, in the helicopter).

09. Day 09: Pronouns & Ownership (My, Mine, His, Hers, Theirs)

How do you say who owns what without stumbling? Compare Possessive Adjectives with Possessive Pronouns:

Person With a Noun (Adjective) Standalone at End (Pronoun) Clear Example
I my car This car is mine. "That is my laptop." ↔ "That laptop is mine."
You your phone This phone is yours. "Is this your bag?" ↔ "Is this bag yours?"
He his jacket This jacket is his. "I found his notebook."
She her idea The idea was hers. "That was her presentation." ↔ "The credit is hers."
They their team The victory is theirs. "It is their responsibility."

10. Day 10 & 11: The 4 Big Parts of Speech for Speaking

Forget textbook definitions. You only need to recognize 4 roles when speaking:

1. NOUN (चीज़ / नाम)

What is it?

laptop, coffee, office, Delhi, Abhimanyu
2. VERB (काम / हरकत)

What is happening?

runs, codes, explains, learns, listens
3. ADJECTIVE (खूबसूरती / खासियत)

What kind of thing is it?

fast, expensive, reliable, exhausted, kind
4. ADVERB (काम कैसे हुआ)

HOW was it done?

quickly, smoothly, politely, immediately
Put them together in one sentence:
"The smart (Adjective) developer (Noun) solved (Verb) the bug quickly (Adverb)."

11. Day 12: Expressing Likes, Dislikes, Preferences & Opinions

In conversation, don't just say "I like this" or "I don't like this" over and over. Use these natural conversational upgrades:

Feeling Upgraded Natural Expression Real-Life Example
Love it "I am really into..." / "I am fond of..." "I am really into machine learning and open-source models."
Dislike it politely "It's not really my cup of tea." "Action movies aren't really my cup of tea; I prefer documentaries."
Preference "I prefer X over Y" / "I'd rather..." "I prefer working from home over commuting every day."
Sharing Opinion "In my perspective..." / "From my experience..." "In my perspective, practical coding beats watching lectures."

12. Day 13: Polite Requests — Could You & Would You

In English culture, blunt imperatives sound aggressive. Notice the transformation:

Blunt / Bossy ❌ Polite & Professional ✅ When to Use
"Give me that file." "Could you please share that file with me?" Emails, Slack, WhatsApp
"Open the window." "Would you mind opening the window?" Cabs, meeting rooms
"Repeat what you said." "Pardon me, could you please repeat that?" Audio calls, bad connection
"Wait for 5 minutes." "Could you please give me a moment?" Counter desk, customer service
"Would you mind..." Golden Rule: When someone asks "Would you mind helping me?", the polite affirmative answer is: "Not at all! Happy to help." (Because 'mind' means feel bad — so 'Not at all' means 'I don't mind at all!').

13. Day 14: Tenses Made Simple — Present Indefinite vs. Continuous

This is where 80% of English learners stumble. Let's make it crystal clear:

Simple Present (रोज की आदत / सच)

Things you do regularly, as a routine, or universal facts.

"I drink tea every morning."
"The sun rises in the east."
"He works at Microsoft."
Present Continuous (जो अभी आँखों के सामने चल रहा है)

Things happening RIGHT NOW at this exact moment.

"I am drinking tea right now."
"He is coding a new feature."
"It is raining outside."
The "Stative Verbs" Trap: You CANNOT put -ing on verbs of thinking, feeling, or senses!
• ❌ "I am knowing him." → ✅ "I know him."
• ❌ "I am understanding." → ✅ "I understand."
• ❌ "I am having two laptops." → ✅ "I have two laptops."

14. Day 15: Past & Future Tenses for Storytelling

To tell stories about yesterday or plans for tomorrow, you only need two structures:

1. The Simple Past (Second Form of Verb — V2):

Whenever talking about finished actions yesterday, last week, or in 2024, use V2:

  • go → went: "I went to the store yesterday."
  • meet → met: "I met an old friend last night."
  • buy → bought: "I bought a new keyboard."
  • call → called: "She called me in the afternoon."

2. The Future (Will vs. Going to):

"Will" (Instant Decision / Promise)

"I will call you in 5 minutes."

"Going to" (Already Planned Future)

"I am going to visit my parents this weekend."

15. Phase 2 Quick-Revision Cheatsheet

Your Week 2 Power Rules:

  • Prepositions: IN for countries/months/years; ON for days/surfaces/walkable transit; AT for exact time and spots.
  • Ownership: "My car" ↔ "The car is mine".
  • Politeness: Always start requests with "Could you please..." or "Would you mind...".
  • Routine vs. Right Now: "I work" (routine) vs "I am working" (right now).
  • Yesterday's Story: Always drop into past verb form V2 (went, met, saw, bought).
