100 Days of Deep Learning: Philosophy, Prerequisites & Roadmap

In this inaugural lecture, Professor Nitish Singh breaks down the fundamental flaw in modern AI education: treating deep neural networks as black boxes. He establishes the first-principles philosophy of the course, lays out the 4 mathematical pillars required for true mastery, and walks through the entire 84-video roadmap from biological neurons to modern Transformers.

Watch Official Lecture on YouTube ↗

01. The Professor's Teaching Philosophy: Scratch-First Learning

Professor Nitish begins the video with a direct provocation to aspiring data scientists and machine learning engineers:

The Professor's Opening Thesis

"Anyone can import Keras, write four lines of model.add(Dense()), and run model.fit(). That does not make you a Deep Learning Engineer; that makes you a software user. A real Deep Learning engineer knows what the matrix dimensions look like during backpropagation, why the loss surface oscillates under high momentum, and how the gradients are mathematically derived using the multivariable chain rule."

The pedagogy of this 100-day series is built on three strict learning rules:

  1. Handwritten Mathematical Proofs First: Every single algorithm (Perceptron learning rule, gradient descent update, cross-entropy loss, backpropagation chain rule, convolution stride formula) is derived on paper before touching code.
  2. NumPy Scratch Implementation: Before importing high-level frameworks like TensorFlow, Keras, or PyTorch, we implement the forward pass, backward pass, and weight update routines in pure Python and NumPy from absolute scratch.
  3. Production Framework Implementation: Once the internal mechanics are completely de-mystified, we rebuild the system using industry-standard libraries (Keras, TensorFlow, PyTorch) and benchmark performance on real-world datasets.

02. The "Black Box" Trap in Deep Learning Interviews

Nitish shares his observation from interviewing dozens of data science candidates for high-paying roles:

  • Surface Knowledge: Candidates can easily recite definitions: "CNN is used for images, RNN is used for text, and Adam is the best optimizer."
  • Interview Failure Point: The moment the interviewer asks: "Can you write down the backpropagation chain rule for a 2-layer network on this whiteboard?" or "Why does Adam optimizer have bias-correction terms in the denominator during the first few epochs?", 90% of candidates freeze.

This course is specifically structured to eliminate that gap by building visceral mechanical intuition for every hyperparameter, weight matrix, and activation curve.

03. The Four Mathematical Pillars Required

Deep Learning is essentially computational applied mathematics. The professor emphasizes that you do not need a PhD in mathematics, but you must master four key branches:

1. Linear Algebra

Scalars, vectors, matrices, and tensors. Vector dot products (geometric projection and similarity), matrix multiplication dimensionality rules ($M \times K \cdot K \times N = M \times N$), matrix transpose, and norms ($L_1, L_2$).

2. Multivariable Calculus

Partial derivatives ($\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$), the gradient vector ($\nabla L$), and above all, the Chain Rule: $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial x}$, which forms the computational backbone of backpropagation.

3. Probability & Statistics

Random variables, Gaussian (Normal) distributions, mean and variance, maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), Bayes theorem, and entropy / cross-entropy loss metrics.

4. Mathematical Optimization

Convex vs non-convex loss functions, local vs global minima, saddle points, ravines, learning rates ($\eta$), and numerical gradient descent step formulations ($w_{new} = w_{old} - \eta \frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$).

04. The 100-Day Curriculum Trajectory (84 Lectures)

Nitish lays out the chronological progression of the 84 videos across 6 major developmental blocks:

Block Videos Core Subject Matter Milestone Deliverable
Block 1: Perceptrons & Foundations Videos 01 – 07 Biological vs artificial neurons, Perceptron trick, Heaviside step, loss functions, XOR failure. NumPy Perceptron from scratch
Block 2: Multi-Layer Perceptrons & ANNs Videos 08 – 20 Forward propagation, Backpropagation derivation, Vanishing gradients, Gradient Descent variants. NumPy MLP & Keras Churn / MNIST
Block 3: Optimization & Hyperparameter Tuning Videos 21 – 39 Dropout, L1/L2 Regularization, Batch Normalization, Momentum, NAG, AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam, Keras Tuner. Production Neural Net Tuning Suite
Block 4: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) Videos 40 – 54 Biological vision (Cat experiment), 2D Convolutions, Padding, Pooling, LeNet-5, Backprop in CNNs, ImageNet, Transfer Learning. Cat vs Dog Classifier & VGG16 Transfer Learning
Block 5: Sequential Models & RNNs Videos 55 – 66 Sequential data, RNN forward pass, BPTT, Vanishing gradients, LSTM internal gates, GRUs, BiLSTMs. IMDb Sentiment Analysis & Next-Word Predictor
Block 6: Attention & Transformers Videos 67 – 84 History of LLMs, Seq2Seq, Bahdanau/Luong Attention, Self-Attention math & code, Multi-Head Attention, Encoder, Decoder, Inference. Full Transformer Architecture Implementation

06. Tools, Compute & Environment Setup

The recommended development stack for the 84-video series:

  • Python 3.9+: Core language runtime.
  • NumPy & Matplotlib: For scratch derivations and mathematical plotting.
  • TensorFlow & Keras: Primary production framework for building ANNs, CNNs, and RNNs.
  • PyTorch: Secondary comparative framework for dynamic graph executions and Transformers.
  • Hardware: Local CPU/GPU or free Google Colab with NVIDIA T4 / V100 GPU instances for model training in later blocks.
