Cloud Computing & Microsoft Azure Basics

What is this module about? The foundational principles of modern cloud computing and Microsoft Azure: how virtualization transformed physical servers, why companies switched from upfront capital spending (CapEx) to operational spending (OpEx), how Microsoft's global fiber network powers 60+ regions, and how to structure resources using Azure's strict 4-level management hierarchy.
Why is this critical for engineers? Every enterprise application, cloud native architecture, and modern AI solution relies on understanding shared responsibility, latency-aware region selection, and fault-tolerant multi-zone deployments.

01. What is Virtualization? (The Core Mental Model)

The Software Engineer's Real-World Problem:

Imagine you have a personal laptop running Windows (or macOS). You write software that must run and be tested on both Linux and Windows. Or perhaps you simply want to learn Linux commands and practice shell scripting.

What are your practical options?

  • Buy a separate physical laptop just for Linux? Unnecessary, costly, and inconvenient.
  • Dual-boot your laptop? Requires restarting every time you switch systems, partitions hard drives permanently, and risks breaking your Windows bootloader.
  • The Engineering Solution: Virtualization! Run Linux right inside your Windows or Mac environment as an isolated, lightweight software computer.

How Virtualization Works: Carving "Mini Computers"

Your physical host laptop has real hardware: for example, 8 GB of RAM and 100 GB of Storage. Through virtualization, you install a specialized piece of software called a Hypervisor (such as Oracle VirtualBox on Windows, or Parallels Desktop on macOS).

The Hypervisor divides your physical hardware into independent, isolated "mini computers":

🖥️ Mini Computer 1 (Linux VM)

Allocated 2 GB RAM • 20 GB Disk from host.
Runs Ubuntu Linux in a completely sandboxed environment.

💻 Mini Computer 2 (Test VM)

Allocated 2 GB RAM • 10 GB Disk from host.
Runs another test operating system or sandbox.

Because these mini computers are generated purely by software rather than separate hardware boxes, we call them Virtual Machines (VMs).

🛡️ The Zero-Risk Isolation Guarantee: If your Linux virtual machine crashes, runs out of memory, or gets infected with malware, it has zero impact on your host operating system. Your primary Windows/Mac machine remains 100% safe, untouched, and operational.

Types of Hypervisors: Type 1 (Bare-Metal) vs. Type 2 (Hosted)

A Hypervisor (Virtual Machine Monitor) is the specialized software layer that coordinates virtual machines by sharing physical hardware:

Hypervisor Category Where It Installs Real-World Examples Primary Industry Usage
Type 2 (Hosted) On top of a primary desktop OS (Windows/Mac). Oracle VirtualBox, Parallels Desktop, VMware Workstation. Student practice, local testing, cross-platform software development.
Type 1 (Bare-Metal) Directly on physical server hardware without any host OS (acts like a mini-OS kernel). Microsoft Hyper-V, VMware ESXi, Citrix XenServer. Cloud Hyperscalers (Azure, AWS) slicing enterprise servers into rentable cloud VMs.

02. What is Cloud Computing?

Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, and analytics—over the internet ("the cloud") with pay-as-you-go pricing.

The Real-World Analogies: Electricity & The City Bus

⚡ The Electricity Grid Analogy:

When you plug your phone charger into the wall socket, you do not build your own coal power station or manage electrical generators. A utility company manages the power plants, and you simply turn on the switch and pay for the exact kilowatt-hours consumed. Cloud computing works identically: Microsoft builds the multi-billion dollar datacenters; you simply consume computing power by the minute.

🚌 The City Bus vs. Private Car (Cloud Deployment Models):

Public Cloud (City Bus): Anyone can purchase a ticket and ride. Highly affordable and accessible because the route and vehicle are shared among passengers traveling together.
Private Cloud (Self-Drive Rental / Owned Car): Reserved exclusively for your organization. Maximum privacy and bespoke control, but you bear 100% of the cost for insurance, fuel, and garage parking even when it sits idle.
Hybrid Cloud (City Bus for Commutes + Car for Weekends): Taking public transit for everyday high-volume travel (public cloud for web frontends) while keeping a private vehicle for sensitive family trips (on-premises private datacenter for confidential banking/health records).

Real-World Case Study: Building a Food Delivery App (Zomato / Tomato)

To understand how individual Azure cloud services cooperate to run an enterprise application, consider designing a food delivery startup like Zomato from the ground up:

Application Function Azure Cloud Service Operational Purpose
Backend REST APIs Azure Virtual Machines / ACA Executes order routing, restaurant search algorithms, and coordinates third-party payment gateways.
Menu & Dish Photos Azure Blob Storage Stores millions of high-resolution food images, restaurant PDF menus, and delivery driver verification documents in an infinitely scalable object store.
Orders & Customer DB Azure SQL / PostgreSQL Persists user profiles, historical order transaction logs, live cart items, and restaurant listings with ACID guarantees.
Push Notifications Azure Notification Hubs Broadcasts real-time alerts ("Your order is out for delivery!") simultaneously to iOS, Android, and driver mobile apps.
Dinner Rush Autoscaling VM Scale Sets / KEDA Automatically spins up 15 additional compute nodes during peak dinner hours (7:30 PM – 10:30 PM) and scales back down at midnight to save money.
24/7 Automated Support Azure AI Bot Service Provides an automated customer assistance chatbot that answers delivery status queries and issues automated refunds without human agent delay.

The 5 Core Characteristics of Cloud (NIST Definition):

  1. On-Demand Self-Service: You can spin up 100 Linux servers at 2:00 AM using a web portal or CLI command without waiting for human approval.
  2. Broad Network Access: Services are available over the internet from phones, laptops, and servers anywhere worldwide.
  3. Resource Pooling: Physical hardware is dynamically allocated and shared among thousands of customers ("multi-tenancy") with complete cryptographic and kernel-level isolation.
  4. Rapid Elasticity: Systems scale out automatically when millions of Black Friday shoppers visit, and contract instantly when traffic drops.
  5. Measured Service: Every second of CPU usage, byte of network bandwidth, and gigabyte of storage is metered like a utility bill.

03. The Shared Responsibility Pyramid (IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS)

Cloud computing is divided into three primary service models. With each step up the pyramid, Microsoft manages more of the underlying complexity, allowing developers to focus purely on business logic.

🍨 The Ice Cream Parlor Mental Model:

IaaS (Empty Shop in a Mall): You rent bare commercial space. You must purchase freezers, install counters, hire workers, and formulate recipes. Full customization, but heavy administrative overhead.

PaaS (Equipped Franchise Parlor): Freezers, soft-serve dispensers, utility electricity, and POS registers are pre-installed and serviced by the landlord. You only bring your unique syrup recipes and serve customers.

SaaS (Automated Vending Machine): You tap your debit card and take your ice cream. You manage zero hardware, zero ingredients, and zero machinery.

⚖️ Cloud Shared Responsibility Model SaaS • PaaS • IaaS • On-Premises
SaaS
You Manage (Customer)

Customer data, user identity, access permissions, MFA, and client devices.

Microsoft Manages (Azure)

Application code, runtimes, OS updates, hypervisors, physical datacenters & networking (e.g. Office 365, PowerBI).

PaaS
You Manage (Customer)

Application source code, database schemas, API configurations, and user secrets.

Microsoft Manages (Azure)

OS patching, web server software, auto-scaling, high availability, and hardware (e.g. Azure App Service, Azure SQL).

IaaS
You Manage (Customer)

Operating System (patching/updates), installed runtimes, firewall rules, and application code.

Microsoft Manages (Azure)

Physical hypervisors, host blade servers, datacenter cooling, power grids, and physical racks (e.g. Azure VMs).

On-Prem
You Manage (100%)

Every single layer from real estate, power generators, cables, switches, to OS and software.

Microsoft Manages

None (traditional private datacenter).

The Pizza Analogy:

  • Traditional On-Premises: You make pizza completely from scratch at home. You buy flour, knead dough, source tomatoes, own the gas oven, and wash the dishes.
  • IaaS (Take & Bake): You buy a prepared raw pizza from the supermarket. The vendor prepared the crust and cheese; you provide the kitchen oven and dining table.
  • PaaS (Pizza Delivery): You order a cooked pizza delivered to your doorstep. You provide the dining table and drinks; the restaurant handles the kitchen, baking, and recipe.
  • SaaS (Dining at a Restaurant): You walk into a pizzeria. You sit down, enjoy the meal, pay the bill, and leave. You don't know or care what brand of oven was used.

04. CapEx vs. OpEx Economics

Why are CFOs and enterprise executives moving workloads to the cloud? It comes down to corporate financial accounting:

Financial Model Capital Expenditure (CapEx) Operational Expenditure (OpEx)
Payment Timing Huge upfront cash payment ($100,000+ for server hardware). Zero upfront cost. Monthly pay-as-you-go bill ($50 - $5,000).
Depreciation Hardware depreciates over 3-5 years and becomes obsolete. Deductible immediately in the current tax year as a business expense.
Business Agility Very slow. Ordering new server blades takes 6-12 weeks. Instant. Spin up or terminate 500 servers in 60 seconds.

Key Infrastructure Definitions:

  • Datacenter: A single physical building packed with server racks, backup diesel generators, cooling towers, and biometric security gates.
  • Availability Zone (AZ): One or more physically separated datacenters within a single region. Each AZ has independent power, independent chilling systems, and independent networking. A flood or electrical fire in Zone 1 does not impact Zone 2 or Zone 3!
  • Azure Region: A geographical area containing multiple Availability Zones connected via a private, ultra-low latency (<2ms) fiber optic network (e.g., East US, Central India, North Europe).
  • Paired Regions: Microsoft connects every Azure region to another region within the same geography at least 300 miles away (e.g., East US is paired with West US; Central India is paired with South India). If an entire region experiences a catastrophe, services fail over cleanly to the paired region.

06. The 4-Level Azure Hierarchy

To keep millions of cloud assets organized, secure, and auditable, Azure enforces a strict 4-level nesting tree:

🏗️ The 4-Level Azure Management Hierarchy Governance • Billing • Lifecycle
Level 1: Management Groups

Governs enterprise access, compliance policies, and budgets across multiple subscriptions.

Level 2: Subscriptions

The primary billing boundary and resource quota limit container (e.g. "Production Subscription", "Dev/Test").

Level 3: Resource Groups

Logical container sharing the exact same lifecycle. Deleting the Resource Group wipes all child assets!

Level 4: Resources

Individual deployed cloud services (Virtual Machines, Storage Accounts, VNets, SQL Databases).

⚠️ The Golden Resource Group Rule:

Every single Azure resource must live inside exactly one Resource Group. If you delete the Resource Group, all resources inside it are deleted immediately. For students and engineers experimenting with cloud tools, creating a dedicated Resource Group (e.g. rg-student-demo) and deleting it after your lab session is the ultimate secret to avoiding surprise cloud bills!

07. Setting Up Your Azure Free Tier Account

Microsoft offers a generous free tier program for students and developers:

  1. Navigate to azure.microsoft.com/free.
  2. Sign in using a personal or corporate Microsoft account.
  3. Verify your identity with a phone number and debit/credit card. (Microsoft charges a temporary $1 authorization hold that is immediately refunded; you will never be auto-charged unless you manually upgrade to Pay-As-You-Go).
🎁 What You Receive in the Azure Free Tier:
  • $200 USD Credit (approx. ₹19,130 INR) to use freely within your first 30 days.
  • 12 Months of Popular Free Services, including 750 hours of Linux B1s VM, 750 hours of Windows B1s VM, and 5 GB of LRS Blob Storage.
  • 55+ Always-Free Services, including Azure App Service (F1 free tier), Azure Functions (1,000,000 free executions/month), and Microsoft Entra ID.

🛡️ Built-in Spending Protection (Zero Surprise Bills)

Azure accounts created on the free trial automatically have a Spending Limit of ₹0 / $0 above free credits enabled by default. When your $200 trial credit expires or runs out after 30 days, Azure automatically stops and deallocates your active VMs and pauses running services instead of silently charging your credit card! You will only ever be charged if you explicitly log into Cost Management and manually choose to remove the spending limit.
